# The slingshot argument

**Question(s)**:
    Does the slingshot argument show that there can be at most one fact, and that the correspondence theory therefore collapses?
    'If every true sentence corresponds to a fact, then every true sentence corresponds to the same fact.' Critically discuss.

# Truth-maker theory

**Question(s)**:
    Must every truth have a truth-maker?
    Is truth-maker theory a more defensible alternative to the correspondence theory of truth?
    What, if anything, makes negative existential statements (e.g. 'There are no unicorns') true?

# Kripke's theory of truth and the fixed point

**Question(s)**:
    Does Kripke's fixed-point construction give a better treatment of the Liar than Tarski's hierarchy of languages?
    Can a language coherently contain its own truth predicate?

# Dialetheism and the law of non-contradiction

**Question(s)**:
    Should we accept that some contradictions are true?
    'The price of dialetheism is too high to pay.' Discuss.
    Is the law of non-contradiction itself in need of justification, and if so, can it be justified non-circularly?

# Bivalence and excluded middle

**Question(s)**:
    Are bivalence and the law of excluded middle the same principle?
    Are there any sentences that are neither true nor false? If so, what follows for classical logic?
    Can one consistently endorse the law of excluded middle while rejecting bivalence?

# Pragmatist theories of truth

**Question(s)**:
    Is truth what works?
    'Truth is whatever survives ideal inquiry.' Is this a satisfactory account of truth?
    Critically assess the pragmatist conception of truth.

# Identity theory of truth

**Question(s)**:
    'A true thought is identical with the fact that makes it true.' Is the identity theory of truth defensible?
    Does the identity theory of truth offer a genuine alternative to the correspondence theory, or is it a notational variant?

# Truth as the aim of assertion and belief

**Question(s)**:
    'To assert is to present as true.' What, if anything, does this tell us about the nature of truth?
    Is truth the constitutive norm of belief?
    Can a deflationist account of truth do justice to the role truth plays in assertion?

# Anti-realism and the rejection of bivalence

**Question(s)**:
    Does Dummett's anti-realism give us good reason to abandon bivalence?
    'A statement can be true only if it is in principle knowable to be true.' Discuss.

# Revenge and the limits of formal solutions to the Liar

**Question(s)**:
    Do all formal solutions to the Liar paradox face revenge versions of the problem?
    'Any consistent theory of truth that is expressively rich enough to be interesting will face a revenge paradox.' Critically discuss.
