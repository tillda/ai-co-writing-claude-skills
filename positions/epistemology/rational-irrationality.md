# Rational Irrationality (UK ch. 17)

Most persistent disagreement on politics, religion, and contested philosophy is not driven by difficulty or ignorance but by *non-epistemic belief preferences* — people find some beliefs more pleasant or socially useful and accept them at low individual epistemic cost. Becoming rational is a moral project, not just a cognitive one.

**Applicable to:** rational irrationality, motivated reasoning, doxastic preferences, automatic bias, becoming rational
