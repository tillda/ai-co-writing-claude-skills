# Moral Knowledge Is Prior to the Categorical Imperative

Kant's foundational story makes the categorical imperative the source of all moral knowledge: pure practical reason generates the test, the test generates the verdicts. The story is the wrong way around. The CI is a useful test of maxims because we already intuitively grasp basic moral truths; it ratifies what we already know. Cutting the foundational claim does not damage Kant's first-order ethics; it strengthens it by relocating the evidence to where the evidence actually lies.

- **What the CI cannot do.** Generate first-order moral content from nothing. The standard objection — many wicked maxims pass universalisation, many morally neutral ones fail it — has bite precisely because we have to assess universalisability cases by appeal to *prior* moral judgments about what would and would not be acceptable. Kant's own derivations (false promising, helping others) work only when we silently import the wrongness or the importance.
- **What the intuitionist takes from Kant.** The CI in both formulations captures something real: universalisation tracks the agent-neutral structure of moral demands; the humanity formula tracks the agent-centred constraint that persons cannot be reduced to mere means. Both are *summaries* of intuitive moral knowledge, not *derivations* of it from a non-moral starting point.
- **The Kantian gain.** This blocks the standard objection that Kant has no resources to answer the egoist who refuses to enter the moral standpoint. The egoist's refusal does not threaten the CI's test-status; it just shows the egoist is not engaging with the intuitive evaluative facts the CI is testing maxims against. Kant overpromised on foundations and is better defended on humbler ground.

**Usage:** Use when the prompt presses Kant's *foundational* claims (deriving morality from pure reason, freedom-morality reciprocity, the deduction in *Groundwork* III). Compatible with `universal-law-formula.md` and `humanity-formula.md`, which take the CI's content seriously without committing to Kant's foundational story.
