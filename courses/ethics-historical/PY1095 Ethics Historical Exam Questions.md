
# Proposed modules

These are identified modules of the course, taken form syllabus. These modules are numbered and the numbering should be preserved in further referencing. These might help in grouping/organizing questions into major themes. Each modile can have multiple topics of inquiry / of questions:

    1. Plato
    2. Aristotle
    3. Hume
    4. Kant
    5. Mill

# 2020 

Explain and assess Plato's claim in the *Republic* that justice is a virtue.
What does Thrasymachus mean by saying that justice is what is to the advantage of the stronger? Is he right?
Does Socrates give a convincing account of who should rule in the city-state?
Does Aristotle have a coherent account of flourishing (*eudaimonia*) in the *Nicomachean Ethics*?
Explain and assess Aristotle's claim that virtue is the mean between a pair of vices that lie at the extreme ends of a scale.
What does Hume mean by saying that reason is 'inert'? Is he right?
Does Hume offer a convincing account of the 'artificial' virtues?
Explain and assess Kant's claim that the only thing that is good without qualification is a good will.
Does Kant offer a convincing account of the ethics of lying?
Does Mill provide a successful reconciliation between general utility and justice?
'As between our own happiness and that of others, utilitarianism requires us to be as strictly impartial as a disinterested and benevolent spectator' (MILL). Does Mill provide a convincing case for this claim?

# 2021

Explain and assess Socrates' claim in Plato's *Republic* that justice is a kind of social harmony.
Is Plato's claim in *Republic* that justice requires the rule of reason in the soul consistent with the claim that all citizens can be just?
Explain and assess Aristotle's claim that virtue is a mean between a pair of vices.
What place does contemplation have in Aristotle's conception of a happy human life?
Explain and assess Hume's argument that moral judgments do not arise from reason.
Does Hume offer a convincing account of the distinction between natural and artificial virtue?
What does Kant think is good without qualification? Is he right?
Is Kant right to claim that all moral requirements are categorical imperatives?
Explain and assess Kant's claim that the possibility of acting morally is one and the same as the possibility of acting freely.
Is Mill right that Utilitarianism provides the best account of justice as ordinarily conceived?
Explain and assess Mill's account of conscience as the ultimate internal sanction of all morality.
# 2022

Explain the significance of the discussion of Gyges's ring in Plato's *Republic*.
Does Plato's *Republic* offer a convincing account of the just city state?
Explain and assess Aristotle's account of what it is to act contrary to one's best practical judgment.
Does Aristotle's ethics imply an implausible account of the function of human life?
Explain and assess Hume's claim that reason is 'inert'.
Does Hume offer a convincing account of the relation between virtue and pleasure?
Explain and assess Kant's account of acting from duty.
Is there a defensible formulation of Kant's Categorical Imperative?
What does Kant mean by saying that freedom and self-legislation are reciprocal concepts? Is he right?
Was Mill a Rule Utilitarian? Should he have been?
Explain and assess Mill's proof of the principle of utility.

# 2023

What, according to Plato, is the relationship between justice in the state and justice in the individual? Is he right?
Does Plato's Socrates convincingly show that someone who is just is better off than someone who is unjust?
Does Aristotle provide a compelling account of happiness (*eudaimonia*) in *Nicomachean Ethics*?
Explain and assess Aristotle's doctrine of virtue as the mean between two extremes.
Does Hume offer a convincing account of the relationship between reason and passion?
Explain and assess Hume's response to 'The Sensible Knave'.
What is the most plausible formulation of Kant's Categorical Imperative? Does it work?
Explain and assess Kant's account of the motive of duty in *Groundwork*.
Was Mill an Act-Utilitarian? Should he have been?
How does Mill think we are able to distinguish between 'higher' and 'lower' pleasures? Is he right that there is such a distinction?
Is Mill's 'proof' of Utilitarianism sound? Was it meant to be?

# 2024

Evaluate the following statement. "Plato's account of justice relies on an implausible theory of the soul."
Why does Plato think that acting morally benefits both the individual and society? Is he right?
Can Aristotelian ethics be sustained if no unified purpose for humanity can be identified, and why/why not?
Does Aristotle think that akrasia is possible? Is it possible to act akratically?
What explanatory work, if any, does Hume's distinction between natural and artificial virtue do?
Why does Hume think that our moral judgments are not based on *reason*? Assess one argument Hume gives for this conclusion.
What does it mean to "treat humanity, whether in your own person or in that of another, always as an end and never as a means only" (Kant, *Foundations of the Metaphysics of Morals*)? How much of our moral conduct can this principle capture?
What does Kant mean when he says that, by acting morally, we act freely? Is he right?
What problem did Mill attempt to solve by appeal to higher and lower pleasures? Does his appeal work?
Does the utilitarian have a satisfactory response to the objection that "it is exacting too much to require that people shall always act from the inducement of promoting the general interests" (Mill, *Utilitarianism*)?
Appeal to ideas from one of the philosophers studied on this module to examine the ethical status of online piracy (the illegal downloading and distribution of copyrighted work).
