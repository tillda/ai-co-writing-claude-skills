# Skeptical Scenarios Are Bad Theories (Unfalsifiability)

The dreaming, brain-in-a-vat, and deceiving-demon hypotheses are *theories about why we have the experiences we have*. As theories they are unfalsifiable in the probabilistic sense: they make no predictions about the course of experience — the deceiver, the scientists, or the dreaming mind can produce *any* sequence whatsoever, so every observation is equally compatible with them. The Real World Hypothesis, by contrast, predicts what we actually find: a coherent sequence of experiences that fit together and obey stable regularities. Evidence equally consistent with two theories supports neither; evidence *predicted* by one and merely *permitted* by the other supports the predicting theory. So the Real World Hypothesis is supported by our evidence; the skeptical hypotheses are not. Cf. Huemer *Knowledge, Reality, and Value* §6.3.4 [227-254].

The argument is Bayesian, not Popperian: a hypothesis that no possible observation could raise the probability of has no claim on belief, regardless of any demarcation criterion. This blocks the standard skeptical move at the first step. The skeptic argues from "I cannot prove I'm not dreaming/envatted/demon-fooled" to "therefore my perceptual beliefs are unjustified", but conceivability is not enough to put a rival on the table for theory-comparison — the skeptic owes a reason to take their hypothesis seriously, and "you can't rule it out" is not such a reason once unfalsifiability is on the table.
