# Proposed modules

These are identified modules (with some of their main reading sources) of the course, taken form syllabus. These modules are numbered and the numbering should be preserved in further referencing. These might help in grouping/organizing questions into major themes. Each modile can have multiple topics of inquiry / of questions:

    1. Doubt
        First Meditation (Rene Descartes)
        Proof of an External World  (G. E. Moore)
    2. Self
        Second Meditation: Of the Nature of the Human Mind (René Descartes)
        Descartes Myth (Gilbert Ryle)
    3. Tragedy
        Of Tragedy (David Hume)
        The Pleasures of Tragedy (Susan L. Feagin)
    4. Dilemma
       Moral Dilemmas (E. J. Lemmon)
       Moral Dilemmas Revisited, extracts (Philippa Foot)
       Costs of Tragedy (Martha C. Nussbaum)
    5. Friendship
        Nicomachean Ethics Book VIII (Aristotle)
        Epistemic Partiality in Friendship (Sarah Stroud)
    6. Equality
        Idea of Equality (Bernard Williams)
        Anarchy, State and Utopia (Robert Nozick)
    7. Identity
        Essay Concerning Human Understanding (John Locke)
        Self and the Future (Bernard Williams)
    8. Freedom
        Prize Essay on the Freedom ofthe Will (Arthur Schopenhauer)
        Asymmetrical Freedom (Susan Wolf)
    9.  Consciousness
        What Is It Like to Be a Bat? (Thomas Nagel)
        The Hornswoggle Problem (Patricia Churchland)
    10. Causality
        Treatise of Human Nature, Book I extracts from Part III (David Hume)
        Causality and Determination (G. E. M. Anscombe)
    11. Qualities
        An Essay Concerning Human Understanding (John Locke)
        Principles of Human Knowledge and Three Dialogues between Hylas and Philonous (George Berkeley)
