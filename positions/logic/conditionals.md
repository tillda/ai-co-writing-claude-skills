# Conditionals (Selective Non-Propositionalism)

Conditionals come in two kinds: those that express propositions and those that don't. Well-formed conditionals where the conventions of use track a real connection between antecedent and consequent ("if you strike this match, it will light", "if it rained, the streets are wet") express propositions in the ordinary way. The paradoxes of material implication ("if the moon is cheese, 2 + 2 = 5", "if Trump is German, Paris is in France") fail to express propositions: the conventions of use don't pick out determinate content, and the "truth" the material conditional ascribes is cheap vacuous satisfaction, not a real claim about the world.

This is **selective non-propositionalism**. It is the same recipe used elsewhere in this set — the Liar, the truth-teller, vague sentences in the Sorites range — applied to a fourth class of sentences exhibiting deficient meaning.

The argumentative path:

- **The paradoxes of material implication.** *A* → *B* is classically true iff ¬*A* ∨ *B*. This generates well-known absurdities: false-antecedent conditionals are vacuously true; true-consequent conditionals are true regardless of antecedent; strengthening of the antecedent always succeeds; or-to-if always works. Ordinary speakers reject these inferences flatly. This is data, not noise.
- **Gricean rescue fails on a use-based theory of meaning.** Jackson and Grice say the paradoxes are *true* but pragmatically misleading. That forces ordinary speakers to be wrong about their own use of "if". On the conceptualist view (see `wittgensteinian-conceptualism.md`), the diagnosis is unavailable — speakers' use is what gives "if" its content, and competent speakers reject the paradoxes as truths, not merely as misleading.
- **Wholesale Adams overshoots.** Adams and Edgington say *all* indicatives are non-propositional and that assertibility goes by *P*(*B* | *A*). Lewis's triviality result confirms you can't combine the Adams thesis with universal propositionality — but the paradoxes are not the only conditionals. "If you strike this match, it will light" makes a determinate claim about the disposition of the match; treating it as merely a conditional credence dissolves content that is genuinely there.
- **The deficient-meaning recipe.** The paradoxes exhibit the same failure as the Liar (over-constrained meaning), the truth-teller (under-constrained), and vague sentences (mismatch with continuous reality): the conventions of use do not deliver determinate content. The material conditional "satisfies" them vacuously — true cheaply, via empty satisfaction — but vacuous satisfaction is not real content. So they are not propositions; truth and falsity do not apply.
- **Selective non-propositionalism is the right shape.** Conditionals where the conventions of use track real content (causal, evidential, structural) express propositions and have ordinary truth-conditions. Conditionals where they don't (paradoxes of material implication, antecedent-failure cases, vacuous satisfaction) are deficient. Adams is right about the deficient cases and wrong to generalise.
- **Counterfactuals fall under the same recipe.** "If I had struck this match, it would have lit" expresses a proposition because there is a determinate modal claim about the match's disposition. "If the moon had been cheese, 2 + 2 would have been 5" is deficient — vacuously satisfied, no real modal claim. Worlds-talk is idiom for the underlying modal fact (see `primitive-modality.md`), not what makes the counterfactual true.
- **Lewis's bombshell does not bite.** The triviality result wrecks the combination of wholesale Adams + universal propositionality. Selective non-propositionalism is not in that combination: Adams's recipe applies only to the deficient cases, where there was no proposition to have a probability anyway, and standard cases keep ordinary truth-conditions.

Crucial qualification: where a conditional expresses a proposition, classical logic applies to it normally — modus ponens, modus tollens, contraposition all work. The deficient cases are simply outside the propositional domain. Restricting the *scope* of classical logic to proposition-expressing sentences is the same move used in `classical-logic.md` and `sorites-paradox.md`.

**Usage:** Default conclusion when the prompt asks about the truth-conditions of indicative conditionals, the paradoxes of material implication, the Adams thesis, the Lewis triviality result, or whether conditionals express propositions. Engage all three rivals (Gricean material conditional, wholesale Adams, possible-worlds semantics) on their own terms before arriving at selective non-propositionalism. The unifying deficient-meaning recipe across the Liar, truth-teller, Sorites, and the conditionals paradoxes is examiner-friendly when noted explicitly.
