# Locke's argument for the primary/secondary distinction

**Question(s)**:
    Should we accept Locke's argument for the distinction between primary and secondary qualities?
    What distinction do Boyle and Locke make between primary and secondary qualities? Explain and assess two arguments, given by Boyle and/or Locke, for making the distinction.

# Berkeley's rejection of the distinction

**Question(s)**:
    On what grounds does Berkeley reject the distinction between primary and secondary qualities? Are they good grounds?
    Does Berkeley succeed in showing that primary qualities exist only in the mind?
    'An idea can be like nothing but an idea.' (Berkeley) How does Berkeley argue for this claim? What conclusions does Berkeley draw using this claim?

# Is there a distinction between primary and secondary qualities at all?

**Question(s)**:
    Is there a distinction between primary and secondary qualities? Relate your answer to Locke or Berkeley, or both.

# Cases of differing appearance — Locke vs Berkeley

**Question(s)**:
    Both Locke and Berkeley discuss cases in which one and the same object appears different to different perceivers or to the same perceiver under different conditions. What implications do Locke and Berkeley derive from such cases? Whose reasoning is more persuasive?
