# Conventional Reference (Against the Necessary *A Posteriori*)

Names and natural-kind terms pick out via the **role grasped at acquisition**, not via rigid attachment to a bare bearer. "Hesperus" picks out the bright thing in the evening sky. "Water" picks out the watery stuff around here. "Aristotle" picks out the man baptised with this name in our linguistic community and tracked through its conventions of use. Reference is **conventional and use-based**, not metaphysically rigid. As a consequence, Kripke's category of "necessary *a posteriori*" dissolves: each example is a contingent empirical claim about which thing fills a role, dressed up by sliding to a vacuous self-identity behind it.

This position rests on the broader Wittgensteinian view of concepts (see `wittgensteinian-conceptualism.md`). The argument here applies that view to names, natural-kind terms, and the modal categories Kripke claimed to discover.

The argumentative path:

- **Kripke's category requires rigid designation.** Kripke's argument runs: (i) identity is necessary — *a* = *b* → □(*a* = *b*); (ii) names and natural-kind terms rigidly designate the same thing in every possible world; therefore (iii) "Hesperus = Phosphorus", "water = H₂O", and the like are necessarily true wherever true, even though discovered empirically. The necessity is real; we just had to do empirical work to find it. This is the "third box" — the necessary *a posteriori*.
- **Rigid designation fails on a use-based theory of meaning.** The meaning of a word is what speakers acquired through use, and use only delivers roles, never bare bearers (see `wittgensteinian-conceptualism.md`). A speaker introducing "Hesperus" while looking at the evening sky has cognitive contact only with "the bright thing in the evening" — not with "Venus stripped of every appearance and property". So "Hesperus" cannot rigidly attach to Venus *as such*; it can only attach to the role grasped at acquisition. The semantic relation Kripke needs (a non-cognitive lock between a name and a bare bearer that survives every counterfactual variation) is not available on any theory of meaning where meaning is constituted by use.
- **The apparent necessity dissolves.** "Hesperus = Phosphorus", on the role-account, reads as "the bright thing in the evening = the bright thing in the morning" — a contingent empirical truth, true here and false in worlds where the morning and evening roles are filled by different planets. The "necessary" reading is generated by silently substituting "Venus = Venus", which is vacuously necessary and tells us nothing. Kripke's third box is built from this slide between two different claims.
- **Same for natural-kind terms.** "Water" was acquired by interaction with rivers, lakes, rain — the watery stuff. That stuff happens, contingently, to be H₂O. In a world where the watery role is filled by XYZ, "water" correctly designates XYZ. So "water = H₂O" is contingent *a posteriori*, not necessary *a posteriori*. Putnam's Twin Earth scenario is not a counterexample but a confirmation: Earthlings and Twin Earthlings share the meaning of "water" (the role); their differing extensions reflect differing fillers — exactly what role-semantics predicts.
- **Origin essentialism falls with rigid designation.** Kripke's argument that you couldn't have had different parents depends on rigid designation of "you" and "those parents" across worlds. Strip that out, and the argument collapses: in a world where the role of "this person" is filled by someone with different origins, that person is me on the role-reading. Necessity-of-origin is a downstream consequence of Kripke's semantics, not an independent metaphysical insight, and falls when the semantics fails.
- **Naming theories are parasitic on usage.** Causal-historical theories, descriptive theories, cluster theories, and Frege's Sense / Reference structure all look successful in some range of cases. They look successful when they happen to track the actual usage pattern of competent speakers. The work is being done by the use, not by the theory. It is entirely possible that some baptism-and-transmission story agrees with how 99% of people use names, but this is a coincidence — the theory just picks out the usage pattern well, and the usage selected the theory among its competitors, not the other way round (see `wittgensteinian-conceptualism.md`). No naming theory is the *source* of meaning; it is a partial *description* of how use works.
- **Linguistic intuition adjudicates the dispute.** We assess theories of reference by running them past intuition on cases — "would Aristotle still be Aristotle if he had been a fisherman?", "is the watery stuff on Twin Earth water?" — not by deriving theorems from a formal framework and insisting the conclusions hold against intuition. The methodology itself shows that use, mediated by intuition, is the source of truth. If we instead held the rigid-designator theory dogmatically, we should be willing to insist (against ordinary judgement) that "Hesperus = Phosphorus" is informationally empty because it states "*X* = *X*". We are not willing to insist on that; the unwillingness is evidence that use, not theory, is what fixes meaning.

Crucial qualification: this position rejects Kripke's modal *third box*; it does not reject the underlying logic of identity. *a* = *b* → □(*a* = *b*) is fine. The disagreement is over what "Hesperus" tracks across worlds, not over what identity does. Identity is necessary; rigid designation is what's optional, and on the conceptualist view it is not available.
