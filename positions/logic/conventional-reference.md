# Conventional Reference (Against Natural-Kind and Origin Essentialism)

Reference works at two layers. At the **reference layer**, a proper name with a successfully baptised bearer rigidly designates that bearer in every counterfactual circumstance — Kripke is right about this much. At the **content layer**, what a speaker grasps is the role the term plays in linguistic practice — the conventions and patterns of use through which the term was acquired. The two layers do different work: the reference layer underwrites the necessity of identity statements between co-referring names; the content layer explains cognitive significance, attitude reports, empty-name cases, and the acquisition of competence.

This split makes peace with Kripke on proper-name identities such as "Hesperus = Phosphorus" — they are necessary *a posteriori*, true in every world where Venus exists — while still letting use-theory handle Frege's puzzle (the two names express different roles, so the identity is informative), empty names (no bearer, no rigid hook, meaning-deficient by use-theory), and substitutivity failures in attitude contexts (the cognitive content differs even when reference matches).

What does *not* survive the concession is the extension of Kripke's framework to natural-kind terms and to origin essentialism. Those targets remain.

This position rests on the broader Wittgensteinian view of concepts (see `wittgensteinian-conceptualism.md`). The argument here applies that view to the *content* layer of names and natural-kind terms, while granting the reference layer for proper names with existing bearers.

The argumentative path:

- **Concede rigid designation for proper names with existing bearers.** A speaker baptising "Aristotle" picks out *that man*; in counterfactual circumstances the name tracks him, not whoever happens to fill some descriptive role. The intuitions Kripke marshals — Aristotle could have been a fisherman, Gödel could have stolen the proof, Feynman could be the man you can't otherwise pick out — are correct. Identity of name with name is necessary at the reference layer: where "Hesperus" and "Phosphorus" both rigidly designate Venus, the identity is true in every world where Venus exists.

- **Cognitive content is still use-based.** What a speaker grasps when she acquires "Hesperus" is not "Venus stripped of every appearance and property" but the role *the bright thing in the evening sky*. The content layer does the explanatory work for: Frege's puzzle (two names, two roles, one bearer — informativeness comes from the role-difference, not from the rigid co-reference); attitude reports (substitutivity fails because content differs even when reference matches); empty names (Vulcan: the role under-constrains, no bearer to lock onto, sentence is meaning-deficient); acquisition (children learn roles, not bare bearers); intuition-based dispute resolution (we judge cases by tracking competent use, not by deriving from a formal theory).

- **Natural-kind terms do not pattern with proper names.** "Water" was acquired by interaction with rivers, lakes, rain — the watery stuff. The competence speakers acquire is competence with the *role*, not a rigid lock on a chemical structure they have no cognitive contact with. Putnam's Twin Earth scenario is meant to show the opposite, but it actually confirms the role-account: Earthlings and Twin Earthlings share the meaning of "water" (the role); their differing extensions reflect differing fillers — exactly what role-semantics predicts. The widespread intuition that "Twin Earth water is not water" is itself a piece of role-tracking — once told the chemistry, speakers extend the role with a chemical condition. So "water = H₂O" is contingent *a posteriori*, not necessary *a posteriori*. The asymmetry with proper names is principled: a name is introduced by ostension on a particular individual; a natural-kind term is acquired by repeated interaction with a class of stuff, and that class is fixed by the conventions of use, not by a baptism on a hidden essence.

- **Origin essentialism does not follow from rigid designation.** Even granting that "Aristotle" rigidly designates the man across worlds, it is a further metaphysical claim that *those particular parents* are essential to him. Rigid designation tells us how the name *tracks*; it does not tell us what is *essential* to the bearer. The argument from rigidity to necessity-of-origin needs an extra premise about what constitutes the individual — a premise that use-theory denies. Speakers track Aristotle as a person; the parental origins are background, not constitutive. So necessity-of-origin falls even though rigid designation stands.

- **Naming theories are parasitic on usage.** Causal-historical theories, descriptive theories, cluster theories, and Frege's Sense / Reference structure all look successful when they happen to track the actual usage pattern of competent speakers. The work is being done by the use, not by the theory (see `wittgensteinian-conceptualism.md`). No naming theory is the *source* of meaning; each is a partial *description* of how use works. The two-tier picture above is in the same boat: it describes what use delivers — a rigid bearer-link at the reference layer plus a role-grasping content at the content layer — it does not legislate it.

- **Linguistic intuition adjudicates the dispute.** We assess theories of reference by running them past intuition on cases — "would Aristotle still be Aristotle if he had been a fisherman?", "is the watery stuff on Twin Earth water?" — not by deriving theorems from a formal framework and insisting the conclusions hold against intuition. The methodology itself shows that use, mediated by intuition, is the source of truth. Kripke's intuitions on names align with the conceded reference layer; his intuitions on natural kinds and origins overshoot, and the role-account explains the data better.

Crucial qualification: this position rejects the *extension* of Kripke's framework to natural-kind necessity and origin essentialism; it accepts rigid designation for proper names with existing bearers and the necessity of the identities that follow. Identity is necessary; rigid designation for proper names with existing bearers is granted. What's denied is that the same machinery generalises to "water = H₂O" and "Aristotle's parents are essential to him".

**Usage:** Default conclusion when the prompt asks about natural-kind essentialism, origin essentialism, or whether Kripke's framework extends beyond proper-name identity to natural kinds and origins. When the prompt asks specifically whether names rigidly designate or whether "Hesperus = Phosphorus" is necessary, present this as a *qualified concession*: yes for proper names with existing bearers (reference layer), but the cognitive content is still role-based (content layer) — so Frege's puzzle is solved by the content layer, and the natural-kind / origin extensions do not follow. When the prompt is on Frege's puzzle, substitutivity, or propositional attitude reports, defer to `names-conventional-content.md` instead. When the prompt is on modality more broadly (what worlds are, what makes modal claims true), defer to `primitive-modality.md` and invoke this position only briefly. Always engage Kripke's actual arguments — modal (Aristotle / fisherman), epistemic (Gödel / Schmidt), semantic (Feynman) — before objecting; the examiner expects the canonical positions to be taken seriously, and on the proper-name reference layer they are now substantially accepted.
