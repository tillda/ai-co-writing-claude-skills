# Ideal Client Profile

## Demographics

- **Role**: Examiner in an undergraduate Analytic Philosophy programme
- **Location**: United Kingdom
- **Institution type**: University offering undergraduate philosophy
- **Domain**: Analytic philosophy

## What the Examiner Values

### Primary values

- Conceptual clarity — precise use of terms, clear definitions, no vagueness
- Demonstrated understanding of argumentation — not just WHAT positions exist, but WHY something supports or refutes a claim
- Genuine engagement over landscape surveys — taking the argument somewhere rather than listing views
- A clear thesis stated early

### Desired depth

The level of a sharp, enthusiastic undergraduate who grasps the main and most well-known arguments in analytic philosophy, with sparks of an opinionated approach

### Opinionated approach

Some visible positioning is welcomed and rewarded — the essay should show a thinker who cares about the topic, not a neutral reporter

## What the Examiner Penalises

### Content problems

- Advanced academic opinions or obscure digressions that would be unusual for an undergraduate to encounter through normal study — this looks suspicious rather than impressive
- Mere surveying of positions without engaging with the arguments behind them
- Being too assertive without providing supporting argument
- Not addressing the asked topic or question directly

### Style problems

- Dry, purely formal academic prose sustained for long stretches — formal arguments must be woven together with clear, accessible explanations
- Poor essay structure

### Scope rule

Non-standard or non-mainstream arguments should only appear when the question explicitly calls for them and specifies which ones to use

### Phrases that signal quality

- The reason this matters is ...
- This can be objected to on the grounds that ...
- The strongest version of this argument holds that ...
- However, this faces the following difficulty ...
- In support of this, we can note that ...
