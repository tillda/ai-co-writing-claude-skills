# Ethics: Historical Perspectives (PY1095)

Five-philosopher survey: Plato, Aristotle, Hume, Kant, Mill. Each module corresponds to one philosopher and the relevant primary text. Guttenplan's subject guide is the connective tissue and supplies essential and further reading. The exam questions cluster cleanly by philosopher and rarely cross modules, so prompts should normally resolve to a single module's primary text plus its commentary. The 2024 paper introduced cross-module questions ('appeal to ideas from one of the philosophers to examine X'); for those, pick the philosopher whose framework best handles the case and treat them as a normal single-philosopher prompt.

## 1. Plato

The *Republic*, focused on books I, II and IV. Justice as a virtue of the soul, the soul/state analogy, the challenge of Thrasymachus and Glaucon (including Gyges' ring), the tripartite soul, and Plato's claim that the just life benefits the just person. Use for prompts on Plato's defence of justice, the relation between justice in the state and justice in the individual, whether the soul/state analogy works, and Thrasymachus' definition of justice as the advantage of the stronger.

**Sources:**
- Guttenplan guide ch. on Plato
- *Republic* bks I, II, IV (Shorey or Jowett translation; Stephanus pagination)
- Brown 'Plato's Ethics and Politics in The Republic' (SEP)
- Vlastos 'Justice and Happiness in Plato's Republic'
- Sachs 'A Fallacy in Plato's Republic'
- Annas *An Introduction to Plato's Republic*

## 2. Aristotle

*Nicomachean Ethics*, with focus on *eudaimonia*, the function argument, the moral and intellectual virtues, the doctrine of the mean, *akrasia*, and the contemplative life. Use for prompts on whether Aristotle has a coherent account of flourishing, whether virtue is the mean between two vices, the function-of-man argument, whether *akrasia* is possible, and the role of contemplation in the happy life.

**Sources:**
- Guttenplan guide ch. on Aristotle
- *Nicomachean Ethics* bks I-II (function, virtue, mean), VII (akrasia), X (contemplation) — Ross or Crisp translation, Bekker numbers
- Kraut 'Aristotle's Ethics' (SEP)
- Hursthouse *On Virtue Ethics* (Oxford, 1999)
- Broadie *Ethics with Aristotle* (Oxford, 1991)

## 3. Hume

*Treatise of Human Nature* book III and *Enquiry Concerning the Principles of Morals*. The inertness of reason, the slave-of-the-passions doctrine, the moral sentiments, the natural/artificial virtue distinction, justice as artificial, and the response to the sensible knave. Use for prompts on why Hume thinks reason is inert, whether moral judgments arise from reason or sentiment, the natural/artificial distinction, why justice is artificial, and the sensible knave.

**Sources:**
- Guttenplan guide ch. on Hume
- *Treatise* bk III pts I-II (esp. T 3.1.1 'Moral Distinctions Not Deriv'd from Reason')
- *Enquiry Concerning the Principles of Morals* §§I-III, IX, App. I
- Cohon 'Hume's Moral Philosophy' (SEP)
- Mackie *Hume's Moral Theory*

## 4. Kant

*Groundwork of the Metaphysics of Morals*. Good will, acting from duty, the categorical imperative (universal law, humanity-as-end, kingdom of ends formulations), the freedom/morality reciprocity claim, and the application to lying. Use for prompts on what is good without qualification, the formulations of the CI and whether any is defensible, treating humanity as an end never merely as a means, the relation between freedom and moral law, and Kantian ethics of lying.

**Sources:**
- Guttenplan guide ch. on Kant
- *Groundwork* sections I-III (Akademie 4:393-463; Gregor or Wood translation)
- Korsgaard *Creating the Kingdom of Ends* (Cambridge, 1996)
- O'Neill *Constructions of Reason: Explorations of Kant's Practical Philosophy* (Cambridge, 1989)
- Wood *Kantian Ethics* (Cambridge, 2008)

## 5. Mill

*Utilitarianism*. The greatest happiness principle, the higher/lower pleasures distinction, the proof of utility (ch. IV) and its alleged fallacy, conscience as internal sanction, justice and rights reconciled with utility (ch. V), and the act/rule debate. Use for prompts on Mill's proof of utility, higher vs lower pleasures, whether Mill is a rule or act utilitarian, the reconciliation of utility with justice, and the demandingness objection.

**Sources:**
- Guttenplan guide ch. on Mill
- *Utilitarianism* chs. I-V (Crisp ed. or Hackett ed.)
- Crisp *Mill on Utilitarianism*
- West *An Introduction to Mill's Utilitarian Ethics*
- Driver 'The History of Utilitarianism' (SEP)
