# Against Substance Dualism (Physicalism)

I am against substance dualism — Cartesian dualism, the soul theory, and any view that posits a non-physical *substance* alongside the physical. I am strongly in favour of some form of physicalism.

This is not eliminativism. There are genuinely non-physical things — *processes* and *patterns* like consciousness, life, fire, software. These are real. But they are *reducible*: they "run on" physical hardware. Fire is not a substance distinct from the burning fuel; it is what the fuel is doing under combustion. Consciousness is not a substance distinct from the brain; it is what the brain is doing. The hardware/software framing captures this: the software is real, but it cannot exist without hardware to run on, and what makes a particular pattern *that* pattern is the physical activity, not some immaterial extra.

The argumentative path:

- **Substance dualism faces the interaction problem.** If mind and body are distinct substances, how do they causally interact? Descartes' pineal-gland answer is unsatisfying; any answer that posits causal contact between physical and non-physical substances either re-physicalises the mental or breaks physical conservation laws.
- **Ryle's category-mistake critique.** Treating "the mind" as an inner non-physical thing alongside the body is the mistake of expecting an extra entity where the activity itself is the explanation. Ryle's Oxford-tour example: looking for "the University" after seeing the colleges, libraries, and quad — the University *is* those things in operation, not a further item. The same goes for the mind: it is the brain's activities, not a further item.
- **Strawson on persons.** A person is a single subject of both physical and psychological predicates ("she is six feet tall and depressed"); carving the person into two substances loses what makes person-ascription work in the first place. The unity of the person is metaphysically prior to the body/mind distinction, not derivative of it.
- **Process-not-substance framing handles consciousness without dualism.** Consciousness is genuinely non-physical *as a process* (you cannot read it off a circuit diagram), but it is not a non-physical *substance*. It is what the physical hardware is doing — same structure as fire (what fuel is doing under combustion) or life (what biological matter is doing under organisation). In neither case do we posit a non-physical fire-substance or life-substance, and we should not posit a non-physical mind-substance either.
- **Why I reject the rivals.** Strict eliminativism (there are no mental states) is implausible: I clearly have experiences. Property dualism (mental properties are non-physical but supervene on the physical) is closer to defensible but adds an explanatory commitment without clear gain over the process-framing. Substance dualism — including Huemer's soul-theory defence in *Knowledge, Reality, and Value* §§12.3.8, 12.4 — multiplies entities unnecessarily and inherits the interaction problem.

In detail: the textbook anti-dualist line is in *Reading Philosophy* ch. 2 — Ryle 'Descartes' Myth' (the category mistake) and Strawson on persons.
